Abstract This is a paper that runs. Its four figures are programs executing in your browser right now: a per-pixel integration of null geodesics around a Schwarzschild black hole; an extrapolation integrator for the three-body problem that measures the growth rate of chaos; a small neural network trained on the spot to transport Gaussian noise onto any shape you draw; and a QR encoder whose Reed–Solomon decoder recovers the text from a symbol you have damaged. Everything from the governing equation down to the pixel is written here by hand, with no libraries and no network requests. Table 1 is a self-check: 24 assertions that run when the page opens, each comparing the running code against an independently obtained result.

1光线在黑洞附近怎么走How light travels near a black hole

图 1 的每个像素都是一条光线。从相机出发、逆着光的传播方向追过去，它只有三种结局：落进视界，像素是黑的；撞上吸积盘，像素取盘在那一点的颜色；逃到无穷远，像素取那个方向的星空。全部物理都在“光线怎么弯”这一件事里。Every pixel of Figure 1 is a ray of light. Followed from the camera against its direction of travel, a ray can end in only three ways: it falls through the horizon and the pixel is black; it hits the accretion disk and the pixel takes the disk's colour at that point; or it escapes to infinity and the pixel shows the sky in that direction. All of the physics lies in one question: how does the ray bend?
